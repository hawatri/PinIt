PinIt Privacy Policy

Last updated: 31 October 2026

PinIt is a notes app for Android made by Kia Hawatri. This policy explains what data PinIt handles and where it goes. In short: your notes stay on your phone unless you choose to back them up, and PinIt has no ads, no analytics and no tracking.

Data stored on your device

Everything you create in PinIt is stored in the app's private storage on your phone. That includes notes, lists, links, contacts, locations, QR codes, app lists, references to images and PDFs, and voice recordings. PinIt does not send this data to the developer or to any server run by the developer.

Optional Google Drive backup

If you sign in with Google and turn on online backup:

  • PinIt stores your Google account name and email address on your device so it can show who is signed in.
  • PinIt uploads a backup file of your notes and voice recordings to a PinIt folder in your own Google Drive. It uses Google's drive.file permission, so it can only see files it created. It cannot read anything else in your Drive.
  • The backup goes from your phone straight to your Google account. The developer never receives a copy.

Google handles sign-in and Drive storage under the Google Privacy Policy. You can sign out in Settings at any time, and you can delete the backup file from your Drive.

Optional offline backup

An offline backup saves a file to your phone's Download/PinIt folder. It stays on your device unless you move or share it yourself.

Permissions and why PinIt asks for them

  • Camera: to scan QR codes. Scanning happens on your device.
  • Contacts: to let you pick a contact for a contact note. Only the contact you pick is saved, in that note.
  • Location: to place your current location on a location note. It is only used when you ask for it.
  • Microphone: to record voice notes. Recordings are saved on your device.
  • Notifications and alarms: to pin notes to your notification shade and fire reminders.
  • Biometrics: to unlock locked notes. Android handles your fingerprint or face data. PinIt never sees it.
  • Accounts: for Google Sign-In, only if you use online backup.

Internet connections

PinIt only goes online for features you use:

  • Link notes: PinIt opens the web page you saved to read its title, description and preview image. That website sees a normal request from your phone. YouTube preview images load from YouTube.
  • Maps: location notes load map tiles from OpenStreetMap, and address search uses Android's built-in geocoding service. These services see your IP address and the map area or address you look up.
  • Backup: online backup talks to Google Drive, as described above.

What PinIt does not do

  • No ads and no advertising IDs.
  • No analytics, crash-reporting or tracking tools.
  • No selling or sharing of your data with anyone.

Children

PinIt is a general-audience app and is not directed at children under 13. It does not knowingly collect personal information from children.

Deleting your data

PinIt (by Kia Hawatri) keeps your data on your phone and, if you turn on online backup, in your own Google Drive. The developer never holds a copy, so there is nothing to request from us: you can delete everything yourself.

  1. One or more notes: on the Home screen or in Archive, long-press a note to select it (tap others to add them), then tap the Delete icon. Swiping a note only archives it.
  2. Everything on your phone: uninstall PinIt, or go to Android Settings, Apps, PinIt, Storage, and tap Clear storage. This deletes all notes, voice recordings, settings, and the saved Google account name and email.
  3. Online backup: in PinIt, tap your profile picture at the top right of the Home screen and choose Sign out. Then open Google Drive and delete the PinIt folder. This removes the backup file of your notes and recordings.
  4. Offline backup: delete the backup files in your phone's Download/PinIt folder.

Deleted data is gone immediately. PinIt keeps no copy and has no retention period. Images and PDFs you attached stay where they were on your phone, because PinIt only stores a link to them.
